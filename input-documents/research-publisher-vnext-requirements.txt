# Research Publisher vNext - Requirements Draft

## 0. Purpose and Architectural Position

### 0.1 Purpose
The Research Publisher shall transform ROS-compliant research artifacts into a usable, navigable, searchable, and explainable research experience without changing the canonical research source.

### 0.2 Canonical Source
ROS Markdown artifacts remain the canonical source of truth.

The publisher shall not require migration of existing ROS Markdown into a new persistence format.

Any internal typed representation shall be derived from the ROS artifacts and shall not replace them as the authoritative record.

### 0.3 Core Architectural Principle
The system shall separate:

- research persistence: ROS Markdown
- domain interpretation: typed F# model
- application/domain state: SDE
- browser/application boundary: Limen
- presentation: native HTML and CSS
- publishing/deployment: generated static or static-first artifacts where practical

### 0.4 Primary Goal
The system shall improve the ability to understand, navigate, inspect, and connect research across documents.

The system shall not merely convert one Markdown file into one HTML page.

---

# 1. Required Standards and Constraints

## 1.1 ROS
The system shall follow ROS rules and conventions.

The system shall consume ROS-compliant research artifacts without requiring changes solely for presentation purposes.

The system shall preserve ROS identifiers, references, evidence links, status fields, metadata, and provenance.

If ROS evolves, the publisher shall support explicit version handling rather than silently assuming compatibility.

## 1.2 State-Directed Engineering / Ordo
The system shall be designed and implemented according to SDE/Ordo principles.

Domain states shall be explicit.

Legal transitions shall be explicit.

Illegal states shall be unrepresentable where practical.

State changes shall occur through defined transitions rather than arbitrary mutation.

Capabilities shall expose actions that are legal in the current state.

Obligations shall expose unresolved or required work.

External effects shall be explicit.

Version and policy checks shall occur at defined boundaries.

The system shall fail clearly when required invariants are violated.

## 1.3 Limen
The browser application shall use Limen as the browser/application boundary.

Limen shall own or coordinate browser-facing state transitions where appropriate.

Business and research-domain logic shall not be implemented in ad hoc JavaScript.

JavaScript shall remain minimal and be limited to browser interop or functions that cannot reasonably be handled through Limen/WASM.

HTML and CSS shall remain native browser concerns.

Routing, history, clipboard, navigation state, and browser events shall follow the established Limen patterns.

## 1.4 F#
The primary implementation shall be F# unless a technical constraint makes that impossible.

Any non-F# component shall require a documented justification.

Shared domain rules, parsing rules, validation rules, and publication rules should be implemented in F# where practical.

## 1.5 Dependency Discipline
External libraries shall be minimized.

A library shall only be introduced when its value clearly exceeds the operational and maintenance cost.

Frameworks shall not be introduced where a small direct implementation is sufficient.

---

# 2. Research Artifact Ingestion

## 2.1 Repository Input
The publisher shall operate over a research repository or a defined research root within a repository.

It shall discover relevant ROS research artifacts according to ROS conventions.

It shall not require a hand-maintained list of every source file unless ROS explicitly requires one.

## 2.2 Existing Artifact Compatibility
Existing ROS-compliant Markdown shall remain publishable without manual conversion.

Legacy-but-valid ROS content shall be supported where practical.

If older ROS versions require transformation, the transformation shall be deterministic, explicit, and testable.

## 2.3 Parsing
The publisher shall parse ROS Markdown into a typed internal representation.

The parser shall preserve:

- document identity
- document type
- headings
- metadata
- relationships
- source references
- evidence references
- findings
- open questions
- experiments
- theories
- journal entries
- decisions
- status
- provenance
- timestamps where present
- version information
- unknown content that should not be discarded

## 2.4 Unknown Sections
Unknown or future ROS sections shall not be silently dropped.

The parser shall preserve unrecognized sections and expose them as unclassified or extension content.

Unknown content shall generate a warning only when appropriate rather than automatically causing publication failure.

## 2.5 Parse Errors
Parse errors shall identify:

- file
- section
- line or location where practical
- expected form
- actual form
- severity
- whether publication can continue

## 2.6 Partial Failure
One malformed artifact shall not necessarily prevent publication of the entire site.

Fatality shall be determined by explicit policy.

The publication summary shall clearly identify skipped, degraded, or invalid artifacts.

---

# 3. Typed Research Model

## 3.1 Runtime Model
The publisher shall build a typed F# runtime representation of the research corpus.

The runtime model shall represent both individual artifacts and relationships across artifacts.

## 3.2 Stable Identity
Every research object that can be referenced independently shall have a stable identifier.

Identifiers shall remain consistent across publications.

## 3.3 Relationships
The model shall support explicit relationships including, where available:

- supports
- contradicts
- derived from
- produced by
- references
- supersedes
- superseded by
- answers
- raises
- validates
- invalidates
- relates to
- depends on
- evidence for
- evidence against

The implementation shall not invent relationships that are not represented in or deterministically derivable from ROS content.

## 3.4 Provenance
Every derived view shall retain a path back to the canonical artifact and, where practical, the specific section or content that produced it.

## 3.5 Derived State
Derived values shall be clearly distinguishable from source-authored values.

Examples include:

- inbound reference count
- outbound reference count
- unresolved dependency count
- evidence coverage
- relationship graph membership
- publication warnings

---

# 4. SDE Domain States

## 4.1 Publication State
Publication shall have explicit states such as:

- NotLoaded
- Loading
- Parsed
- Validated
- ReadyToPublish
- Publishing
- Published
- PublishedWithWarnings
- Failed

Exact names may change, but state semantics shall be explicit.

## 4.2 Artifact State
Each artifact shall have explicit processing state such as:

- Discovered
- Parsed
- Valid
- ValidWithWarnings
- Invalid
- Excluded
- Published

## 4.3 Navigation State
Interactive browser state shall be modeled explicitly, including:

- selected artifact
- selected research object
- selected lens/view
- active filters
- search query
- expansion state where needed
- navigation history
- selected relationship
- selected source/evidence item

## 4.4 Invalid State Prevention
Examples of invalid states that should be prevented:

- selecting an artifact that does not exist
- navigating to a relationship target that cannot resolve without a defined degraded state
- marking publication successful while fatal validation errors exist
- generating a relationship page from unresolved internal identifiers without surfacing the issue
- exposing actions that are not legal in the current state

---

# 5. Validation

## 5.1 ROS Contract Validation
The publisher shall validate ROS research artifacts before or during publication.

## 5.2 Reference Validation
Internal references shall be checked for resolvability.

Broken references shall be surfaced.

## 5.3 Identifier Validation
Duplicate identifiers shall be treated as errors unless ROS explicitly permits them.

## 5.4 Structural Validation
Required sections and metadata shall be validated according to document type and ROS version.

## 5.5 Semantic Validation
Where ROS defines semantic rules, those rules shall be validated.

Examples may include:

- referenced evidence must exist
- superseded items should identify their replacement where required
- closed questions should have resolution data where required
- experiments should expose outcome state where required

## 5.6 Validation Severity
Validation shall classify findings at minimum as:

- information
- warning
- error
- fatal

## 5.7 Validation Output
Validation results shall be usable both by humans and automation.

A machine-readable validation artifact shall be generated.

---

# 6. Research Projections / Views

## 6.1 Projection Model
The publisher shall create multiple views over the same underlying research corpus.

A view shall not require duplication of source content.

## 6.2 Artifact View
Each canonical ROS artifact shall have a readable representation.

## 6.3 Finding View
The system shall provide a finding-centered view that can show:

- finding text
- source artifact
- supporting evidence
- contradictory evidence
- related findings
- related experiments
- related theories
- downstream references
- provenance

## 6.4 Evidence View
The system shall provide an evidence-centered view that can show:

- evidence details
- source
- where it is referenced
- findings it supports
- findings it contradicts
- experiments associated with it
- provenance

## 6.5 Source View
The system shall provide a source-centered view when sources are represented in ROS.

It shall show what claims, evidence, findings, or research objects depend upon the source.

## 6.6 Experiment View
Experiments shall be navigable as first-class research objects.

Where possible, show:

- hypothesis
- method
- result
- linked findings
- evidence
- follow-up work
- unresolved questions

## 6.7 Theory View
Theories shall be navigable as first-class research objects where present.

The view should expose:

- supporting findings
- contradicting findings
- evidence
- experiments
- current status
- related theories

## 6.8 Question View
Open and resolved questions shall be first-class navigable objects where ROS provides them.

## 6.9 Timeline View
The system should provide chronological views when timestamps or journals permit them.

## 6.10 Relationship View
The system shall provide a way to inspect relationships across research objects without requiring users to open every underlying Markdown file.

## 6.11 Research Health View
The system should provide an aggregate health/integrity view containing metrics such as:

- artifacts processed
- validation warnings/errors
- unresolved references
- unresolved questions
- findings without evidence where detectable
- evidence with no consumers where relevant
- contradictions
- superseded items still referenced
- relationship counts

Metrics shall remain descriptive and shall not create unsupported research conclusions.

---

# 7. Navigation

## 7.1 Research-First Navigation
Navigation shall be oriented around research concepts and relationships rather than filesystem structure alone.

## 7.2 Filesystem Access
Users shall still be able to locate the source artifact associated with a rendered item.

## 7.3 Stable URLs
Published research objects shall have stable URLs where practical.

## 7.4 Deep Linking
Users shall be able to link directly to:

- an artifact
- finding
- evidence item
- experiment
- theory
- question
- relevant section or object where stable identity exists

## 7.5 Back/Forward Navigation
Browser history shall work naturally through Limen.

## 7.6 Breadcrumbs
The UI shall expose enough context for users to understand where a research object originated.

## 7.7 Related Items
Relevant related objects shall be discoverable without returning to a central index.

---

# 8. Search and Filtering

## 8.1 Full-Text Search
The published system shall support full-text search across relevant research content.

## 8.2 Structured Search
Where fields are available, users should be able to search or filter by:

- artifact type
- status
- topic
- identifier
- source
- date
- relationship type
- experiment
- finding
- evidence
- unresolved state

## 8.3 Search Results
Search results shall identify:

- object type
- identifier
- title or summary
- source artifact
- matching context where practical

## 8.4 Static Compatibility
Search should work in the simplest practical deployment model.

If a client-side index is used, its size and performance shall be monitored.

A server dependency shall not be introduced solely for search unless static/client-side search becomes demonstrably insufficient.

---

# 9. User Interface

## 9.1 Native Presentation
Use semantic HTML and CSS.

## 9.2 Accessibility
The publisher shall target WCAG 2.2 AA where practical.

Keyboard navigation shall be supported.

Interactive elements shall have appropriate semantics.

Color shall not be the sole method of conveying research status.

## 9.3 Responsive Design
The interface shall work across desktop, tablet, and phone layouts.

## 9.4 Research Density
The interface shall prioritize information density and research comprehension over decorative presentation.

## 9.5 Progressive Disclosure
Large relationship sets and supporting details should be collapsible or progressively disclosed.

## 9.6 Source Visibility
Users shall be able to identify the source artifact behind any important rendered research object.

## 9.7 Print
Important research views should have useful print styles.

## 9.8 Copying
Limen clipboard support shall allow copying stable links, identifiers, excerpts, or citations where appropriate.

---

# 10. Publishing Model

## 10.1 Static-First
The publisher shall prefer static output wherever practical.

## 10.2 GitHub Pages
The publisher shall support GitHub Pages publication.

## 10.3 CI/CD
A GitHub Actions workflow shall be provided for validation, build, and publication.

## 10.4 Build Reproducibility
The same source revision and publisher version shall produce functionally equivalent output.

## 10.5 Publication Manifest
Each publication shall create a manifest containing at minimum:

- publisher version
- ROS version(s)
- source revision/commit
- publication timestamp
- artifact counts
- validation summary
- output version/schema

## 10.6 Failed Publication
Fatal validation or build errors shall prevent publication according to explicit policy.

The last known-good published site should remain available when possible.

---

# 11. Incremental Publishing

## 11.1 Change Detection
The system should determine which artifacts changed between revisions.

## 11.2 Dependency Awareness
If artifact A changes and artifact B has a derived view that depends on A, B's derived projection shall be refreshed.

## 11.3 Full Rebuild
A full deterministic rebuild shall always remain possible.

## 11.4 Correctness Over Optimization
Incremental publication shall not be introduced until correctness of dependency tracking is established.

---

# 12. Machine-Readable Outputs

## 12.1 Research Index
The publisher shall emit a machine-readable research index.

## 12.2 Relationship Index
The publisher shall emit a machine-readable relationship index.

## 12.3 Search Index
The publisher shall emit a search index where required by the frontend.

## 12.4 Validation Report
The publisher shall emit a machine-readable validation report.

## 12.5 Agent Consumption
Outputs should be usable by agents without scraping rendered HTML.

## 12.6 Contract Stability
Machine-readable outputs shall be versioned.

Breaking changes require an explicit upgrade path.

---

# 13. Extension Model

## 13.1 New ROS Artifact Types
The architecture shall permit adding new ROS research artifact types without redesigning the entire publisher.

## 13.2 New Projections
New views/projections shall be addable without altering canonical research artifacts.

## 13.3 Extension Points
Extension points shall be explicit rather than relying on arbitrary runtime hooks.

## 13.4 Unknown Future Metadata
Unknown metadata shall be preserved whenever safe and practical.

---

# 14. Security

## 14.1 Static Content
Generated content shall be treated as untrusted input during rendering.

## 14.2 HTML Sanitization
Embedded HTML or script-capable Markdown content shall not be blindly emitted.

## 14.3 Script Execution
Research artifacts shall not be able to introduce arbitrary executable JavaScript into the published site.

## 14.4 Repository Credentials
Publishing credentials shall never be emitted into generated output.

## 14.5 External Links
External links should be rendered safely and should not gain unnecessary browser privileges.

---

# 15. Performance

## 15.1 Initial Load
The initial published experience should not require loading the full research corpus into browser memory unless corpus size demonstrates that this is acceptable.

## 15.2 Index Loading
Search and relationship indexes should be partitionable if size grows materially.

## 15.3 Navigation
Normal research navigation should feel immediate after initial page/application load.

## 15.4 Large Repositories
The architecture shall not assume the research corpus will remain small.

Performance thresholds shall be measured rather than guessed.

---

# 16. Observability and Diagnostics

## 16.1 Build Diagnostics
The publisher shall expose clear build diagnostics.

## 16.2 Publication Metrics
Track at minimum:

- artifacts discovered
- artifacts parsed
- artifacts published
- parse failures
- validation warnings
- validation errors
- unresolved references
- build duration
- output size
- search index size

## 16.3 Structured Logging
Logging shall follow the standard Echelon Foundry error/logging approach when available.

## 16.4 Debug Output
A diagnostic mode shall provide enough information to understand how a given source artifact became a published projection.

---

# 17. Testing

## 17.1 Unit Testing
Domain, parsing, validation, state transitions, and projection logic shall have thorough unit tests.

## 17.2 Golden Tests
Representative ROS documents should have deterministic expected parse/projection outputs.

## 17.3 Invalid Input Tests
Tests shall cover malformed, incomplete, contradictory, and forward-version input.

## 17.4 Relationship Tests
Relationship resolution and back-reference generation shall be tested.

## 17.5 Navigation Tests
Routing and browser state transitions shall be tested according to Limen conventions.

## 17.6 Publication Tests
The GitHub Pages output shall be testable before deployment.

## 17.7 Regression Corpus
Existing research repositories or a representative sanitized corpus should become a regression suite.

A change that causes existing valid ROS research to disappear or materially mis-render shall fail the regression test.

---

# 18. Backward Compatibility

## 18.1 Existing Research
Existing valid research shall remain publishable.

## 18.2 Existing Links
Existing published URLs should be preserved or redirected where practical.

## 18.3 Existing Publisher
The current publisher shall remain usable until the new publisher demonstrates equivalent or better coverage of the existing corpus.

## 18.4 Migration
Migration shall focus on publisher behavior, not rewriting research artifacts.

## 18.5 No Forced Conversion
No existing Markdown artifact shall require manual conversion solely to work with the new publisher unless it is already invalid under the ROS contract.

---

# 19. Versioning

## 19.1 Publisher Version
The publisher shall expose its own version.

## 19.2 ROS Compatibility
Supported ROS versions shall be explicit.

## 19.3 Output Schema Versions
Machine-readable indexes and manifests shall be versioned.

## 19.4 Upgrade Compatibility
Breaking changes shall require:

- documented rationale
- compatibility analysis
- migration path
- tests for old and new forms

---

# 20. Research Integrity Principles

## 20.1 No Invented Research
The publisher shall not create findings, evidence, conclusions, or relationships that do not exist in the source research.

## 20.2 Derived Presentation
The publisher may derive navigation, indexes, counts, relationships that are deterministic from canonical data, and descriptive health diagnostics.

## 20.3 Provenance
Derived information shall remain traceable to its source.

## 20.4 Contradictions
Contradictory research shall be presented as contradictory rather than silently merged into a synthetic conclusion.

## 20.5 Missing Information
Missing evidence or unresolved references shall be represented as missing/unresolved rather than guessed.

---

# 21. Agent-Friendly Design

## 21.1 No HTML Scraping Requirement
Agents shall not need to scrape rendered HTML to understand the published research.

## 21.2 Stable Machine Contracts
Agents shall have versioned structured indexes for:

- artifacts
- findings
- evidence
- relationships
- validation
- source locations

## 21.3 Source Retrieval
An agent shall be able to determine the canonical source artifact for a published object.

## 21.4 Bounded Context
The system should make it possible for an agent to request or load only the research objects relevant to a task rather than consuming the entire corpus.

## 21.5 Explicit Uncertainty
Where ROS records uncertainty, status, or unresolved work, that information shall remain explicit in machine-readable outputs.

---

# 22. Authoring Feedback

## 22.1 Publisher as Validator
The publisher should help authors identify research quality and structural problems during normal publication.

## 22.2 Actionable Errors
Errors should explain how to correct the ROS artifact rather than merely stating that parsing failed.

## 22.3 Local Validation
The same validation engine used in CI should be runnable locally.

## 22.4 CI Feedback
Pull requests should receive validation results before publication.

---

# 23. Build and Developer Experience

## 23.1 Simple Startup
A repository using the publisher should require minimal setup.

## 23.2 Standard Initialization
The publisher should support the Echelon Foundry package initialization convention if applicable, such as:

npx <package-name> init

or an equivalent standard installer/bootstrap mechanism established for ROS/SDE/Limen tooling.

The runtime implementation does not need to be Node merely because npm/npx is used as a distribution/bootstrap interface.

## 23.3 Configuration
Configuration shall be explicit, small, documented, and versionable.

## 23.4 Convention Over Repetition
ROS conventions should be discovered automatically rather than copied into large configuration files.

## 23.5 Local Preview
Developers shall be able to preview the research site locally before publishing.

---

# 24. Suggested Configuration

The publisher should need only a small amount of configuration, potentially including:

- research root
- output directory
- site title
- base URL
- ROS version policy
- publication mode
- optional theme/custom branding
- feature toggles for experimental projections

Configuration should not duplicate information already available in ROS artifacts.

---

# 25. Themes and Branding

## 25.1 Separation
Research semantics and rendering shall not depend on a particular visual theme.

## 25.2 Default Theme
The publisher shall provide a clean default theme.

## 25.3 Custom Theme
Projects may override CSS or theme tokens without forking the publisher.

## 25.4 Echelon Foundry Theme
An Echelon Foundry-branded theme may exist as a first-party option but shall not be hard-coded into core semantics.

---

# 26. Accessibility and Long-Term Preservation

## 26.1 No JavaScript Requirement for Core Reading
Where practical, core published research content should remain readable even if interactive WASM/Limen functionality is unavailable.

## 26.2 Semantic Output
Generated HTML shall use meaningful document structure.

## 26.3 Durable Publication
A published build should remain understandable as a static artifact independent of the build system.

This preserves the research record over time.

---

# 27. Explicit Non-Goals

The new Research Publisher shall not:

- replace ROS Markdown as the canonical research format
- require migration to a database
- require migration to JSON
- rewrite research content merely for display purposes
- allow the frontend to create unsupported research conclusions
- turn every research relationship into an opaque graph visualization
- introduce a server architecture unless demonstrated requirements demand one
- introduce a large JavaScript framework
- make the published site dependent on GitHub APIs for ordinary reading
- make research unusable outside the publisher
- silently repair malformed research
- hide validation problems to make publication appear successful

---

## Phase 1 - Contract Discovery
- inventory current publisher behavior
- inventory current ROS research artifact types
- identify actual Markdown structures in existing research repositories
- define supported ROS versions

- typed F# parser
- typed research corpus
- relationship resolution

# 29. Questions to Resolve During Requirements Analysis

These should become explicit decisions rather than assumptions.

1. What exact artifact types does ROS currently define for research?
2. Which relationships are explicit in the Markdown today?
3. Which relationships can be derived deterministically?
4. What does the existing publisher generate today?
5. Which current URLs must remain stable?
6. Is publication always repository-local, or can one site aggregate multiple ROS repositories?
7. How large are the largest current research repositories?
8. Does ROS already have a formal schema/version marker?
9. Should publication fail on broken internal references, or publish with warnings?
10. Which research objects already have stable IDs?
11. Should full-text search be entirely static/client-side initially?
12. What current publisher capabilities are considered mandatory for feature parity?
13. What parts of the current UI are specifically ineffective?
14. Does research need access controls, or are published repositories assumed to be public/static?
15. Should the publisher support private GitHub Pages or other deployment targets later?
16. Should one research object be able to appear in multiple topical projections without duplication?
17. Does ROS define citations separately from evidence?
18. How are contradictory findings represented today?
19. How are superseded findings represented today?
20. Is a journal chronological source material, a first-class research object, or both?
21. Do generated diagrams have canonical meaning or are they presentation-only?
22. Should the publisher produce PDF/print exports, or should that remain a separate concern?
23. How should attachments, images, datasets, and other non-Markdown research assets be handled?
24. What is the expected relationship between the Research Publisher and the broader ROS tooling/package?
25. Should publication state and validation status be written back into ROS, or remain purely derived?

# 30. Additional Requirements Worth Considering

## 30.1 Cross-Repository Research
Design identifiers so future research aggregation across multiple repositories is possible even if v1 only publishes a single repository.

Do not require cross-repository support in the first implementation unless needed.

## 30.2 Attachments
Research assets such as images, CSV files, PDFs, raw experiment output, and generated artifacts shall have stable references and provenance.

## 30.3 Citation-Friendly Links
Where stable object identity exists, the publisher should make it easy to copy a durable citation/link to a specific research object.

## 30.4 Diff / Evolution View
A future projection should be able to show how a research object changed over time using repository history.

This should not be required for the first release unless implementation is inexpensive.

## 30.5 Evidence Dependency Inspection
Users should be able to inspect the impact of an evidence item being invalidated or superseded.

Example:
"If EV-017 is removed, which findings or theories depend on it?"

## 30.6 Research Traceability
The architecture should allow tracing:

source -> evidence -> finding -> theory/decision/question

when those relationships exist in ROS.

## 30.7 Bidirectional Navigation
If A references B, the publisher should generally make both directions discoverable even when the source Markdown only encodes A -> B.

The reverse relationship is a derived index, not a mutation of canonical research.

## 30.8 Graceful Degradation
Interactive Limen features shall enhance the research experience but shall not make the underlying research unreadable when unavailable.

## 30.9 Deterministic Derived Data
Any derived relationship or index shall be deterministic and reproducible from the same input revision.

## 30.10 No Hidden Agent Mutation
Agent-assisted authoring or correction, if added later, shall be a separate explicit workflow.

The publisher itself shall remain a reader/interpreter/publisher and shall not silently mutate research artifacts.

---

# 31. Definition of Success

The new publisher is successful when:

- existing ROS research can be published without conversion
- readers can understand relationships across research artifacts without manually traversing Markdown files
- findings, evidence, experiments, theories, questions, and sources can be navigated as research objects
- every important projection remains traceable to canonical ROS content
- validation problems are visible and actionable
- the UI uses Limen and follows SDE/Ordo
- the implementation remains predominantly F#
- static GitHub Pages deployment remains practical
- agents can consume research through structured contracts rather than HTML scraping
- existing research remains readable and durable outside the publisher
- the publisher adds understanding without becoming a new source of truth
